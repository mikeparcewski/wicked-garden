# Kind: operating model

Who does what: the roles, their responsibilities, who decides what, and the cadences that keep the
work moving, as a document a team can adopt. The brief's `Kind: operating model.` line selects this
ref (the **Roles and operating model** card). The skill's three floors still apply.

## Shape

1. **Scope:** the team or programme, and the work this model covers.
2. **Roles:** each role with its purpose and three to six responsibilities. Roles, not people:
   name a person only when a source names them in that role.
3. **Responsibility matrix** (RACI): one row per key activity or decision, one column per role,
   each cell R (does it), A (accountable), A/R (accountable and does it), C (consulted), I
   (informed) or empty.
4. **Decision rights:** which decisions each role makes alone, which need agreement, and how a
   disagreement is escalated, and to whom.
5. **Cadences:** each recurring meeting or ritual with its purpose, frequency, owner, inputs and
   outputs. A cadence with no output is cut.
6. **Interfaces:** how the team takes in work and hands it on (the intake channel, the hand-over
   artefact, who signs off).
7. **Open questions:** what the sources leave undecided.

## Rules

- **Exactly one A per row** (an `A/R` counts as it), and at least one R or `A/R`. A row with two accountable roles is the
  disagreement the model exists to settle: name it under open questions.
- **Observed or proposed, labelled.** What the sources show the team does today (a CODEOWNERS file,
  a process document, a recurring meeting) is cited as current. What you propose is labelled
  "proposed", so a reader can tell the two apart.
- **Fit the team's size.** A five-person team does not get twelve roles or a weekly steering
  committee; keep only what the sources' scale supports.
- **Sized:** two pages unless the brief names a budget (self-check `--pages 2`). The matrix may be
  its own page.

## Never

- assign a named person a role, a responsibility or an authority the sources do not give them;
- present a proposed model as the team's current practice;
- leave a decision with no accountable role.
