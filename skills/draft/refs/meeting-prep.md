# Kind: meeting prep

A briefing a person reads before a meeting, so they walk in knowing what they want out of it, what
the others care about, and what they will be asked. The brief's `Kind: meeting prep.` line selects
this ref. The skill's three floors still apply.

## Shape

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Outcome wanted** | the one decision, agreement or answer the person needs by the end |
| **Who is there** | each attendee the brief or sources name, and what the sources show they care about |
| **Agenda** | items in order, each with a time box that adds up to the meeting's length |
| **Context** | the facts the person must have in hand, each cited (status, numbers, recent changes) |
| **Decisions needed** | each decision, its options, and the person's recommended position with its reason |
| **Likely questions** | the questions the sources suggest, each with a grounded short answer |
| **Risks and sensitive points** | what could derail it, and how to handle it |
| **Pre-reads and follow-ups** | links (cited) and the follow-ups to capture |

## Rules

- **Positions come from sources.** Never assign an attendee a view, a worry or a goal the sources do
  not show. With no source, list the person by role and leave "what they care about" as a question.
- **Answers are short and true.** A likely question with no grounded answer says so: "not known
  yet; offer to follow up by <date>".
- **Recommend, don't decide.** The person's position is a recommendation with its reason; the
  meeting decides.
- **Sized:** one page unless the brief names a budget (self-check `--pages 1 --exact`). The time
  boxes must add up to the meeting length the brief gives; with none given, leave them out.

## Never

- invent attendees, their positions, or the meeting's time and length;
- put a number in the context that the sources do not carry;
- write talking points that promise something the sources do not support.
